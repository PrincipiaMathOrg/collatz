# The normalized one-drop resultant and its exact method ceiling

**Date:** 18 August 2026  
**Mode:** SOLVE  
**Status:** exact denominator equivalence, Smith form, resultant
\(2\)-adic valuation, primitive quotient theorem, and norm-energy no-go.  In the
coprime case, denominator one is exactly one cyclic congruence of modulus
\(D\).  After removing the complete forced power of \(2\), the quotient has
odd norm and coefficient content one.  Neither the norm nor any trace
pairing supplies a second independent divisor.  The resulting energy
inequality is automatic for \(U\geq2\), and an exact high-rise rational
family shows that this route cannot prove a polynomial lower bound for
\(Q\) before the denominator is set equal to one.

## 1. The normalized sum

Put

\[
 N:=\lceil r\log _2 3\rceil,
 \qquad e:=N-r,
 \qquad D:=2^N-3^r.
 \tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
 B_j:=\left\lfloor\frac{jN}{r}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad c_j:=[jN]_r.
 \tag{2}
\]

Choose \(U\geq1\) eligible preterminal two-sites and let \(m_j\) be the
number of chosen sites below \(j\).  Thus \(m_0=0\), \(m_{r-1}=U\), and

\[
 A_j=B_j-m_j,
 \qquad
 \kappa_j:=jN-rA_j=c_j+rm_j.
 \tag{3}
\]

Let \(\theta^r=2\), put

\[
 \delta:=\theta^N-3=2\theta^e-3,
 \tag{4}
\]

and define

\[
 \boxed{
 P_U(X):=2^{U+1}+
 \sum_{j=1}^{r-1}2^{U-m_j}X^{r-c_j}.}
 \tag{5}
\]

When \(c_j=0\), the corresponding term in (5) is interpreted in
\(\mathbf Z[\theta]\) using \(\theta^r=2\).  Finally, let

\[
 E_j:=r(U+1)-\kappa_j.
 \tag{6}
\]

The purpose of this normalization is the identity

\[
 \boxed{
 P_U(\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\theta^{E_j}
 =2^{U+1}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\theta^{-\kappa_j}.}
 \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, for \(j\geq1\), we have

\[
 2^{U+1}\theta^{-\kappa_j}
 =2^{U-m_j}\theta^{r-c_j}.
\]

In other words, the mixed coefficients in (5) become \(r\) monomials of
coefficient one after powers of \(2\) are lifted to powers of \(\theta\).

## 2. The exact denominator class and Smith form

Let \(C\) be the accelerated carry of the one-drop word.  If

\[
 W_\theta:=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\theta^{-\kappa_j},
\]

then reduction modulo \(\delta\) gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 \theta^{N(r-1)}W_\theta
 &\equiv
 \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}
 \theta^{N(r-1-j)+rA_j}\\
 &\equiv
 \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}3^{r-1-j}2^{A_j}
 =C
 \pmod\delta.
 \end{split}
 \tag{8}
\]

Both \(2\) and \(\theta\) are units modulo \(\delta\).  Equation (7)
therefore identifies the exact obstruction represented by \(P_U\).

Let

\[
 d:=\gcd(r,N),
 \qquad r=dr_0,
 \qquad N=dN_0,
 \qquad b:=2^{N_0}-3^{r_0}.
 \tag{9}
\]

We have \(b>0\).  The simultaneous quotient has the explicit form

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbf Z[\theta]/(\delta)
 \simeq
 (\mathbf Z/b\mathbf Z)[T]/(T^d-g),}
 \tag{10}
\]

where \(g^{r_0}=2\) and \(g^{N_0}=3\) in
\(\mathbf Z/b\mathbf Z\).  To see this, first put
\(\eta:=\theta^d\).  Since \(\gcd(r_0,N_0)=1\), the quotient generated by
\(\eta\) is \(\mathbf Z/b\mathbf Z\).  Adjoining \(\theta\) then adds the
monic relation \(\theta^d=\eta=g\), which proves (10).

As an additive group, the right side of (10) is
\((\mathbf Z/b\mathbf Z)^d\).  Consequently, the multiplication matrix of
\(\delta\) on \(\mathbf Z[\theta]\) has Smith form

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{SNF}(M_\delta)
 =\operatorname{diag}(
 \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{r-d},
 \underbrace{b,\ldots,b}_{d}).}
 \tag{11}
\]

Equations (7), (8), and (10) now give

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta\mid P_U(\theta)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 b\mid C.}
 \tag{12}
\]

In particular, if \(\gcd(r,N)=1\), then \(b=D\), and

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta\mid P_U(\theta)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D\mid C.}
 \tag{13}
\]

This verifies the desired denominator-one equivalence in the coprime
case.  It also proves that the quotient lattice contains only one cyclic
\(D\)-class.  There is no hidden collection of \(r\) independent
divisibilities.

For \(d>1\), one copy of \(\delta\) does not represent the full gap

\[
 D=(2^{N_0})^d-(3^{r_0})^d.
\]

For example, take

\[
 (r,N,U)=(6,10,2),
 \qquad S=\{2,4\}.
 \tag{14}
\]

The word is \((1,2,1,1,1,4)\), and

\[
 C=925,
 \qquad b=5,
 \qquad D=295.
\]

In \(\mathbf Z[\theta]\), with \(\theta^6=2\), we have

\[
 \boxed{
 12+6\theta^2+5\theta^4
 =(2\theta^4-3)(52+42\theta^2+33\theta^4).}
 \tag{15}
\]

Thus \(\delta\mid P_U(\theta)\), but \(D\nmid C\).  The other homogeneous
cyclotomic factors of \(D\) are indispensable.

## 3. Exact \(2\)-content of the resultant

We now assume \(\gcd(r,N)=1\).  The residues \(c_j\) are a permutation of
\(0,\ldots,r-1\), so the exponents \(E_j\) are distinct modulo \(r\).  Put

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda:=\min_j E_j
 =r(U+1)-\max_j\kappa_j.}
 \tag{16}
\]

Every index with \(m_j<U\) has \(E_j>r\), while the terminal index gives
\(E_{r-1}=e\).  Therefore,

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda
 =\min_{m_j=U}(r-c_j)
 =r-\max_{m_j=U}c_j,
 \qquad 1\leq\lambda\leq e<r.}
 \tag{17}
\]

Factor (7) as

\[
 P_U(\theta)=\theta^\lambda u,
 \qquad
 u:=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\theta^{E_j-\lambda}.
 \tag{18}
\]

Exactly one exponent in \(u\) is zero, and all other exponents are
positive.  Hence \(u\equiv1\pmod\theta\).  Since

\[
 \operatorname{Norm}(\theta)=\pm2,
\]

we obtain the exact valuation

\[
 \boxed{
 v_2\operatorname{Res}(X^r-2,P_U)=\lambda.}
 \tag{19}
\]

The ordinary polynomial content of \(P_U\) is one, because the terminal
coefficient is one.  Thus (19) is ramified \(\theta\)-content, not a
scalar common factor of the coefficients.  After all of it is removed,

\[
 \boxed{
 R_U^\flat:=
 \frac{|\operatorname{Res}(X^r-2,P_U)|}{2^\lambda}
 =|\operatorname{Norm}(u)|
 \quad\hbox{is odd}.}
 \tag{20}
\]

More generally, without the coprimality assumption, write

\[
 P_U(\theta)=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}a_s\theta^s
\]

in the canonical basis.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 v_2\operatorname{Res}(X^r-2,P_U)
 =\min_{a_s\ne0}\bigl(s+r v_2(a_s)\bigr).}
 \tag{21}
\]

The minimum in (21) is unique because its possible values are distinct
modulo \(r\).  This also explains why ordinary content can occur when
\(d>1\), even though it cannot occur in the coprime case.

## 4. The primitive quotient under denominator one

Let \(x_j=n_j/q\) be the reduced rational orbit of the word, with common
odd denominator \(q\).  Summing the accelerated recurrences gives

\[
 W_\theta
 =\delta\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}x_j\theta^{-\kappa_j}.
 \tag{22}
\]

Combining (7) and (22), we obtain

\[
 \boxed{
 qP_U(\theta)=\delta\Phi_q,
 \qquad
 \Phi_q:=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}n_j\theta^{E_j}.}
 \tag{23}
\]

After removing the ramified factor, put

\[
 \Psi_q:=\theta^{-\lambda}\Phi_q
 =\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}n_j\theta^{E_j-\lambda}.
 \tag{24}
\]

The canonical coefficients of \(\Psi_q\) are positive.  They also have
content one.  Indeed, the exponents in (24) are distinct modulo \(r\), so
each coefficient is a power of \(2\) times one numerator state.  Any odd
common divisor would divide every \(n_j\).  The recurrence

\[
 2^{a_j}n_{j+1}=3n_j+q
\]

would then show that it also divides \(q\), contrary to reduction.

Taking norms in (23) gives the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{
 q^rR_U^\flat=D|\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi_q)|.}
 \tag{25}
\]

For denominator one, this becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 R_U^\flat=D|\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)|,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{content}(\Psi)=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)\ \hbox{odd}.}
 \tag{26}
\]

Equation (26) is the strongest scalar norm consequence of the normalized
sum.  The factor \(D\) has already been consumed once.  The Smith form
(11) forces no further divisor of \(\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)\).

## 5. Trace pairings give the coordinate recurrence

One may try to obtain a second condition by pairing (26) with traces.  We
now show that all monomial trace pairings merely read the quotient
coordinates.

Write

\[
 \Psi=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}b_s\theta^s.
\]

Since the conjugates of \(\theta\) are
\(2^{1/r}\zeta_r^k\), we have

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(\Psi)=rb_0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(\theta^{r-s}\Psi)=2rb_s
 \quad(1\leq s<r).
 \tag{27}
\]

Applying (27) to

\[
 \delta\Psi=u
\]

recovers, coefficient by coefficient, the accelerated state recurrence.
An arbitrary trace pairing is a \(\mathbf Z\)-linear combination of these
coordinate equations.  Therefore, it cannot give a second residue class
independent of (13).  The norm is their determinant, while their product
around the unique exponent cycle is the original closure equation.

## 6. The exact energy inequality is automatic

Put

\[
 \rho:=2^{1/r},
 \qquad \theta_k:=\rho\zeta_r^k.
\]

The exponents \(E_j-\lambda\) are distinct modulo \(r\).  Orthogonality
therefore gives the exact Parseval identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac1r\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}|u(\theta_k)|^2
 =\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}4^{(E_j-\lambda)/r}.}
 \tag{28}
\]

The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and (26) would give

\[
 D^{2/r}
 \leq |\operatorname{Norm}(u)|^{2/r}
 \leq \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}4^{(E_j-\lambda)/r}.
 \tag{29}
\]

However, the \(j=0\) summand alone is

\[
 4^{U+1-\lambda/r}>4^U.
 \tag{30}
\]

For \(U\geq2\), equations (1) and (30) give

\[
 D^{2/r}<9<16<4^{U+1-\lambda/r}.
 \tag{31}
\]

Thus (29) is true for every profile before denominator one is used.  It
cannot exclude any high-rise stratum.

The quotient has the analogous exact identity

\[
 \frac1r\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}|\Psi(\theta_k)|^2
 =\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}x_j^2
 4^{(E_j-\lambda)/r}.
 \tag{32}
\]

Its odd integral norm gives only

\[
 1\leq|\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)|.
\]

The right side of (32) already contains the nonzero constant coefficient
\(x_{j_*}^2\geq1\), where \(E_{j_*}=\lambda\).  Hence this inequality is
also automatic.

For completeness, the global state envelope gives a direct coefficient
bound.  The coefficient attached to \(j\) is

\[
 2^{\lfloor(E_j-\lambda)/r\rfloor}x_j.
\]

Since

\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{E_j-\lambda}{r}\right\rfloor
 \leq U-m_j,
 \qquad
 x_j<4x2^{m_j},
\]

every coefficient is smaller than \(4x2^U\).  Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 |\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)|
 <\bigl(8\sqrt r\,x2^U\bigr)^r.}
 \tag{33}
\]

Combining (33) with the only forced lower bound
\(|\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)|\geq1\) gives a bound weaker than the known
inequality \(x\geq1\).  In
particular, it cannot imply \(Q\geq U^p\) for any positive \(p\).

## 7. An exact high-rise rational obstruction

The full-selection profiles make the method ceiling explicit.  Select
every eligible two-site, so that

\[
 U=e-1,
 \qquad
 a=(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{r-1},e+1).
 \tag{34}
\]

Then

\[
 \kappa_j=ej,
 \qquad
 E_j=e(r-j),
 \qquad
 \lambda=e.
 \tag{35}
\]

It follows that

\[
 P_U(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\theta^{ek}
 =\theta^e\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\theta^{ek}.
 \tag{36}
\]

If \(\gcd(r,N)=1\), then \(\gcd(r,e)=1\), and therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 R_U^\flat=(2^e-1)^{r-1}.}
 \tag{37}
\]

The rational fixed point and its quotient coordinate are

\[
 x=\frac{3^r-2^r}{D},
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 Q:=\frac{x+1}{2}
 =\frac{2^{r-1}(2^e-1)}{D}.}
 \tag{38}
\]

Let \(N/r\) run through the upper continued-fraction convergents to
\(\log _2 3\).  Then

\[
 \gcd(r,N)=1,
 \qquad
 0<N-r\log _2 3<1,
 \qquad
 N-r\log _2 3\longrightarrow0.
\]

For all sufficiently large members, \(x>1\).  The first \(r-1\) branches
then strictly increase because \((3y+1)/2>y\) for \(y>1\), and the final
branch returns to \(x\).  Thus the displayed anchor is strict least.  The
height satisfies

\[
 \frac Ur\longrightarrow\log _2 3-1,
 \qquad
 \frac{U^2}{r}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{39}
\]

The audited linear-form estimate already used in the cycle ledger gives

\[
 x<305r^{143/10}.
 \]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{Q<153r^{143/10}.}
 \tag{40}
\]

Since \(U\asymp r\), equation (40) violates every proposed
denominator-free lower bound \(Q\geq U^p\) with \(p>143/10\), including
the required exponent \(271/10\).

After reduction to a common denominator \(q\), the quotient in (24) is
positive and primitive, and

\[
 \boxed{
 |\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi_q)|
 =\frac{q^r(2^e-1)^{r-1}}{D}.}
 \tag{41}
\]

For all sufficiently large members, \(q>1\).  Indeed, if \(q=1\), then
(38) and the oddness of \(D\) would give \(2^r\mid x+1\), while the
polynomial upper bound gives \(0<x+1<2^r\).

At the small coprime row

\[
 (r,N,e,U)=(11,18,7,6),
\]

we have

\[
 C=175099,
 \qquad D=q=84997,
 \qquad Q=\frac{130048}{84997}<2,
 \tag{42}
\]

and

\[
 \lambda=7,
 \qquad
 R_U^\flat=127^{10}
 =1091533853073393531649.
 \tag{43}
\]

The orbit is strict least, \(U^2/r>2.315385\), the cleared quotient has
content one, and (41) holds exactly.  Thus the primitive normalized norm
structure is compatible with bounded \(Q\) at a genuine high-rise point.
Only the scalar \(q=1\) is missing.

## Conclusion

The normalized polynomial gives a complete answer to the proposed
resultant route.  In the coprime sector,

\[
 D\mid C
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \delta\mid P_U(\theta),
\]

the exact resultant valuation is \(\lambda\), and removal of
\(2^\lambda\) leaves the odd identity

\[
 R_U^\flat=D|\operatorname{Norm}(\Psi)|.
\]

The quotient is positive and primitive, but these properties also hold
after a rational denominator is cleared.  The Smith form contains one
cyclic \(D\)-class, all trace pairings reproduce the coordinate
recurrence, and the exact Parseval bound is automatic.  Therefore this
normalization does not prove \(Q\geq U^p\) and excludes no remaining
high-rise stratum.  A successful continuation must introduce a second
global datum not generated by multiplication by \(\delta\), for example a
genuinely independent prime-power synchronization across the full
denominator.
