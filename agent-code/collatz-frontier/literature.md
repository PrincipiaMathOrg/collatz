# Normalized literature and provenance

This catalog answers two separate questions for every cited source: what is the original source, and what exactly did the local Collatz work take from it? A citation listed as context or analogy is not a proof dependency. The structured records are in [`data/literature.json`](data/literature.json).

## Direct proof inputs and terminology

### `LIT-BARINA-2025`

David Barina, “Improved verification limit for the convergence of the Collatz conjecture,” *Journal of Supercomputing* 81 (2025), Article 810. [DOI 10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0).

Imported use: verification of all starts below (2^{71}), hence a hypothetical nontrivial cycle has minimum state (>2^{71}). Used by `THM-CYCLE-REDUCE-01`.

### `LIT-BERNSTEIN-LAGARIAS-1996`

Daniel J. Bernstein and Jeffrey C. Lagarias, “The (3x+1) conjugacy map,” *Canadian Journal of Mathematics* 48 (1996), 1154–1169. [DOI 10.4153/CJM-1996-060-x](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1996-060-x).

Imported use: the standard (2)-adic parity/conjugacy framework underlying the parity-series comparison. The project’s no-go calculation is its own result.

### `LIT-FERNANDEZ-IBANEZ-2026`

Carlos Fernández and Santiago Ibáñez, “Christoffel words as extremal structures in Collatz dynamics,” [arXiv:2607.24844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24844) (2026).

Imported use: Christoffel/mechanical extremality. The exact denominator classification and deformation analysis are project results.

### `LIT-GARCIA-TAL-1999`

Manuel V. P. Garcia and Fabio A. Tal, “A note on the generalized (3n+1) problem,” *Acta Arithmetica* 90 (1999), 245–250. [DOI 10.4064/aa-90-3-245-250](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-90-3-245-250).

Imported use: generalized-map shell and reciprocal-sum context for `CERT-SHELL-01`.

### `LIT-HERCHER-2023`

Christian Hercher, “There are no Collatz (m)-cycles with (m\le91),” *Journal of Integer Sequences* 26 (2023), Article 23.3.5. [Official article and supplementary files](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL26/Hercher/hercher5.html); [arXiv:2201.00406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00406).

Imported use: a nontrivial cycle has at least (92) local minima. The official page now links a corrigendum dated 14 June 2026. The correction repairs the proof while preserving the stated result; both article and corrigendum must travel together in future citations.

### `LIT-LAGARIAS-1985`

Jeffrey C. Lagarias, “The (3x+1) problem and its generalizations,” *American Mathematical Monthly* 92 (1985), 3–23. [DOI 10.2307/2322189](https://doi.org/10.2307/2322189).

Imported use: standard formulations, parity encoding, and historical/cycle terminology.

### `LIT-ROZIER-TERRACOL-2026`

Olivier Rozier and Claude Terracol, “Paradoxical behavior in Collatz sequences,” *Discrete Mathematics* 349 (2026), 115167. [arXiv:2502.00948v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00948); [DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2026.115167](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2026.115167).

Imported use: stopping/coefficient-stopping terminology, paradoxical sequences, and the relation of first coefficient crossings to cycle exclusion. The project’s canonical dyadic-wrap formulation is separate.

### `LIT-RHIN-1987` and `LIT-SIMONS-DEWEGER-2005`

Georges Rhin, “Approximants de Padé et mesures effectives d’irrationalité,” in *Séminaire de Théorie des Nombres, Paris 1985–86*, Progress in Mathematics 71 (1987), 155–164. [DOI 10.1007/978-1-4757-4267-1_11](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4757-4267-1_11).

John L. Simons and Benne M. M. de Weger, “Theoretical and computational bounds for (m)-cycles of the (3n+1)-problem,” *Acta Arithmetica* 117 (2005), 51–70. [DOI 10.4064/aa117-1-3](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa117-1-3); [updated author-hosted version 1.44](https://math.deweger.net/papers/%5B35a%5DSidW-3n%2B1-v1.44%5B2010%5D.pdf).

Imported use: the cycle notes use the specialization

\[
\Lambda>\exp\{-13.3(0.46057+\log K)\},
\]

which is Lemma 12 of Simons–de Weger, obtained by applying Rhin’s Proposition on p. 160. Future prose should cite this two-source chain, not an unqualified “Rhin estimate.”

### `LIT-TERRAS-1976`

Riho Terras, “A stopping time problem on the positive integers,” *Acta Arithmetica* 30 (1976), 241–252. [DOI 10.4064/aa-30-3-241-252](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-30-3-241-252).

Imported use: stopping time, coefficient stopping time, and finite parity-cylinder context.

### `LIT-WIRSCHING-1998`

Günther J. Wirsching, *The Dynamical System Generated by the (3n+1) Function*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1681, Springer (1998). [DOI 10.1007/BFb0095981](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0095981).

Imported use: standard accelerated-dynamics and inverse-tree background.

### `LIT-YAH-2023`

Emre Yolcu, Scott Aaronson, and Marijn J. H. Heule, “An automated approach to the Collatz conjecture,” *Journal of Automated Reasoning* 67 (2023), **Article 15**. [DOI 10.1007/s10817-022-09658-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10817-022-09658-8); [arXiv:2105.14697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14697).

Imported use: the exact string-rewrite formulation and deleted-subsystem context. One legacy bibliography says “Paper No. 28”; the publisher metadata shows Article 15, which is the normalized value here.

### `LIT-MONKS-ETAL-2013`

Keenan Monks, Kenneth G. Monks, Kenneth M. Monks, and Maria Monks, “Strongly sufficient sets and the distribution of arithmetic sequences in the 3x+1 graph,” *Discrete Mathematics* 313 (2013), 468–489. [DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2012.11.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2012.11.019); [arXiv:1204.3904](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3904).

Imported use: every positive orbit hits \(2\bmod9\), and every nontrivial cycle or divergent orbit hits \(20\bmod27\). These strongly sufficient sets constrain a hypothetical counterexample but do not prove convergence. Used by `THM-STRONG-SUFFICIENT-01` and `SK-RDC`.

### `LIT-MONKS-2025`

Kenneth G. Monks, “On q-analogs of the 3x+1 dynamical system,” *Journal of Integer Sequences* 28 (2025), Article 25.6.8. [Official article](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL28/Monks/monks6.html); [arXiv:2508.10153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10153).

Imported use: Theorem 1 conjugates the family \(T_{A,B}\) on \(\mathbf F_2[[q]]\) to the shortcut map on \(\mathbf Z_2\); Theorem 3 completely classifies polynomial orbits for \(T_{1,1+q^2}\). Conjecture 2, that a conjugacy sends every positive integer to a polynomial, is explicitly equivalent to Collatz. Used by `SK-QPOLY`; the equivalence is not presented as a reduction in difficulty.

### `LIT-ROJAS-ZHU-2022`

J. Maurice Rojas and Yuyu Zhu, “Root repulsion and faster solving for very sparse polynomials over (p)-adic fields,” *Journal of Number Theory* 241 (2022), 655–699. [DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2022.01.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2022.01.013); [arXiv:2107.09173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09173).

Imported use: an explicit (p)-adic sparse-root separation estimate inside a cycle subargument. It is not a Collatz theorem by itself.

### `LIT-BONDAREVA-LUCHIN-SALIKHOV-2018`

I. V. Bondareva, M. Yu. Luchin, and V. Kh. Salikhov, “Symmetrized polynomials in a problem of estimating the irrationality measure of (ln3),” *Chebyshevskii Sbornik* 19 (2018), 15–25. [DOI 10.22405/2226-8383-2018-19-1-15-25](https://doi.org/10.22405/2226-8383-2018-19-1-15-25).

Imported use: an irrationality-measure bound in an auxiliary Diophantine estimate.

## Quantitative and structural context, not terminal dependencies

- `LIT-TAO-2022`: Terence Tao, “Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values,” *Forum of Mathematics, Pi* 10 (2022), e12. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2022.8). This does not exclude one exceptional divergent orbit.
- `LIT-KRASIKOV-LAGARIAS-2003`: Ilia Krasikov and Jeffrey C. Lagarias, “Bounds for the 3x+1 problem using difference inequalities,” *Acta Arithmetica* 109 (2003), 237–258. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa109-3-4); [arXiv:math/0205002](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205002). Their computer-aided difference inequalities prove that at least \(x^{0.84}\) integers below \(x\) reach 1; this is a reverse-tree density theorem, not a universal result.
- `LIT-APPLEGATE-LAGARIAS-2003`: David Applegate and Jeffrey C. Lagarias, “Lower bounds for the total stopping time of 3x+1 iterates,” *Mathematics of Computation* 72 (2003), 1035–1049. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-02-01425-4); [arXiv:math/0103054](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103054). Infinitely many convergent trajectories have total stopping time greater than \(6.14316\log n\), warning against an assumed shallow universal descent depth.
- `LIT-KONTOROVICH-SINAI-2002`: Alex V. Kontorovich and Yakov G. Sinai, “Structure theorem for \((d,g,h)\)-maps,” *Bulletin of the Brazilian Mathematical Society* 33 (2002), 213–224. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s005740200010); [arXiv:math/0601622](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601622). The Wiener-limit theorem rigorously describes typical drift and diffusion for generalized Collatz maps, not every deterministic trajectory.
- `LIT-LAGARIAS-WEISS-1992`: Jeffrey C. Lagarias and Alan Weiss, “The 3x+1 problem: two stochastic models,” *Annals of Applied Probability* 2 (1992), 229–261. [DOI and official Project Euclid page](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoap/1177005779). The forward and inverse random models illuminate stopping-time statistics but do not transfer universal conclusions to the fixed map.
- `LIT-KURTZ-SIMON-2007`: Stuart A. Kurtz and Janusz Simon, “The undecidability of the generalized Collatz problem,” LNCS 4484 (2007), 542–553. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-72504-6_49). This concerns generalized systems, not undecidability of the fixed (3x+1) conjecture.
- `LIT-MONKS-YAZINSKI-2004`: Kenneth G. Monks and Jonathan Yazinski, “The autoconjugacy of the (3x+1) function,” *Discrete Mathematics* 275 (2004), 219–236. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(03)00125-0). (2)-adic context only.
- `LIT-KRAMER-2026`: Oliver Kramer, “Adaptive search in Collatz exponent-code space via (2)-adic and (3)-adic constraints,” [arXiv:2607.10041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10041). Search-method context only.
- `LIT-NIU-2026`: Tong Niu, “Parity vectors and paradoxical sequences in the accelerated Collatz map,” [arXiv:2605.13886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13886). Withdrawn in v2 because Rozier–Terracol v4 already enumerated the sequences and the remaining mediant observation followed routinely from their data; context only, and no retained gate depends on it.
